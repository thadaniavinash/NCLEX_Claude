## For clinician review

1. **PEEP units corrected:** the source says PEEP 5 "mmHg"; PEEP is measured in **cm H₂O**.
2. **Screen 1:** the option "PCO2 50 mm Hg" now reads 51 mm Hg to match the chart. The key (pH, PaCO₂, RR, SpO₂) is the author's; note that **lactate 4.5 mmol/L** (with a positive blood culture and WBC 35.0) is also an urgent sepsis finding, so some reviewers may argue for it.
3. **Antibiotic order:** "Amoxicillin 500 mg IVPB every 12 hours" is kept from the source but is questionable: IV amoxicillin is not available in Canada or the US, and amoxicillin is a narrow choice for gram-negative bacteremia with pneumonia. It does not change any answer (screen 5 keys antibiotics as "next"), but consider replacing it (e.g. with a broad-spectrum IV agent).
4. **Blood culture "gram-negative cocci"** (source) is an unusual organism description (gram-negative rods are far more typical); kept.
5. **SaO₂ 96%** first appears on the screen 5 lab report with the 0900 gases (PaO₂ 75 mm Hg, SpO₂ 87% at 0900); it likely reflects post-intubation values. Kept as the source shows it.
6. **Lab values in SI units** with the author's ranges converted: mEq/L → mmol/L for HCO₃⁻, K⁺, Na⁺ and lactate; magnesium 1.5 mEq/L → 0.75 mmol/L (range 0.75–1.05); WBC 35,000/mm³ → 35.0 × 10⁹/L (4.5–10.5); platelets 250 × 10⁹/L (140–450). Blood gases stay in mm Hg, as MCC prints them.
7. **Screen 5 key** comes from the source's highlighted Key table: start the IV of 0.9% sodium chloride, midazolam, chest X-ray.

## Screen 1 · Recognize cues · `select_n`

**Select the **4** findings that are **most** urgent.**

1. BP 168/90
2. WBC 35.0 × 10⁹/L
3. Lactate 4.5 mmol/L
4. pH 7.20  ✅
5. PaCO₂ 51 mm Hg  ✅
6. PaO₂ 75 mm Hg
7. Respiratory rate 30  ✅
8. Blood culture: gram-negative cocci
9. Pulse oximetry reading 87%  ✅

**Rationale:** The client has signs and symptoms of infection and respiratory failure. The most urgent findings are related to respiratory failure: a pH of 7.20 indicating an acidic state, a PaCO2 above 50 mm Hg, tachypnea (RR 30), and a pulse oximetry reading of 87% on high-flow oxygen. The blood pressure is elevated but is not yet critical.

## Screen 3 · Prioritize hypotheses · `multiple_choice`

**What does the nurse determine is the priority for this client's care?**

1. Administering corticosteroids to reduce inflammation
2. Intubating for mechanical ventilation support  ✅
3. Opening airways with aerosol treatments
4. Treating infection with antibiotics

**Rationale:** Acute respiratory failure is characterized by the lungs' inability to oxygenate properly. The client has had a poor response to maximum supplemental oxygen and now needs mechanical ventilation.

The respiratory failure was most likely caused by pneumonia; treating the pneumonia should take place next. Aerosols and corticosteroids may be incorporated into the treatment plan but are not as critical as intubation.

## Screen 5 · Take action · `highlight_2`

*The nurse has reviewed the Laboratory Results and receives orders.*

**Click to highlight the **3** orders the nurse should implement **first**.**

- Insert urinary catheter
- Suction endotracheal tube as needed
- Titrate oxygen to keep pulse oximetry reading at or above 95%
- Start IV of 0.9% sodium chloride at 75 mL/hr  ✅
- Amoxicillin 500 mg IVPB every 12 hours
- Midazolam 2–4 mg IV push every 1 hour as needed for agitation  ✅
- Acetaminophen 650 mg per rectum every 8 hours as needed for T > 100.8°F (38.2°C)
- Perform 12-lead ECG
- Call for chest X-ray  ✅
- Blood gas in 30 minutes

**Rationale:** A chest X-ray is needed after intubation to confirm optimal endotracheal tube placement. The client does not yet have venous access; this should be established immediately so medications can be given. The BP and heart rate are significantly elevated, and the client is agitated; sedation (midazolam) should be given to decrease agitation and the risk of self-extubation.

Next, an ECG can be done and antibiotics can be given. The oxygen level is above 95%, so adjustments are not needed, and there is no indication that suctioning is needed. The blood gas is not due yet. The urinary catheter can be placed after other treatments.
