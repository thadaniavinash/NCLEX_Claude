## Case Study — NURS 1021 Unit 3 Case Study 1 (pneumonia / possible sepsis, history of AFib)

**Item ID:** `case_1786030000001`. A 78-year-old woman brought to the ED by her adult child with
fever, productive cough, and confusion, history of atrial fibrillation → diagnostic workup
(pneumonia vs. UTI vs. influenza) → deteriorates toward septic shock → sepsis-bundle
treatment → partial improvement.

- **Screen 1** (select 4, "requires immediate follow-up"): vital signs, lung sounds, radial
  pulse characteristics, cough characteristics — **not** capillary refill or client orientation
  (both documented within normal limits on this exam, despite the family's historical concern).
- **Screen 2** (matrix, pneumonia/UTI/influenza): fever → all three; confusion → pneumonia +
  UTI; body soreness → influenza only; cough/sputum → pneumonia only; shortness of breath →
  pneumonia + influenza.
- **Screen 3** (dropdown): "dysrhythmias" — ✅ **confirmed by the source's revealed selection**.
- **Screen 4** (matrix, indicated/not indicated): semi-Fowler's, increase O2, additional VAD,
  and fluid bolus = Indicated; prepare for defibrillation = Not Indicated (client has a pulse).
- **Screen 5** (highlight, "priority orders"): vancomycin, the normal saline bolus, and the
  blood culture/CBC/ABG labs (the sepsis bundle) — ✅ **confirmed by the source's revealed
  highlight**. Not the catheter or the CT scan.
- **Screen 6** (matrix, improved/not changed/worsened): BP and client interacting with family =
  Improved; pale skin tone and pulse oximetry = Not Changed; respirations 36 = Worsened.

## Items I'd most want a clinician's second opinion on

- **Item 88** (VAD insertion technique) — single-select among 2 plausible "correct-sounding"
  options (fist clenching vs. tapping the vein); I went with tapping because current practice
  increasingly discourages repetitive fist pumping (it can skew lab values), but this is a real
  judgment call, not a clean-cut answer.
- **Item 73** (lithium) vs the alternative reading of "5 weeks" for onset — plausible either way
  depending on which source's timeframe a given program teaches.
- **Item 96** (AV shunt, "bruit by palpating") — I read this as testing the bruit/auscultation vs.
  thrill/palpation distinction, but it's possible the item intends this as loosely correct
  phrasing; worth a nursing-instructor's read.
- **Item 71** option 6 (antiemetic p.r.n.) — I excluded it since the client's presenting symptoms
  are chills/back pain, not nausea, but some instructors include symptom-directed comfort
  measures as part of the initial reaction response regardless.
